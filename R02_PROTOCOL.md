# R02 decision protocol

PR #1 head is the baseline. Use the original T02 task, 48-unit architecture, batch size of 64, CPU float32, one thread, original model/feedback/sampler seed offsets and 25-step evaluation interval. Use R01's selected fast rate and AdamW settings. The seven rows in the grid give name, anchor rate, stiffness growth rate and restoring strength. Each intervention changes one coefficient from the original spring control. The two zero settings isolate stiffness-dependent anchor gating from elastic restoration. Lightweight DFA allocates only fast matrices and fixed feedback. The moving-anchor baseline additionally allocates two anchor matrices.

Train each setting on all nine R01 corpus/model pairs. Lock the positive-stiffness, positive-restoration setting and the moving-anchor rate with lowest mean final deployed validation CE, breaking exact ties by listed order. Only after saving the lock, construct the three new corpora and evaluate light DFA, selected anchor, selected full spring, reference spring and AdamW (reuse duplicate settings). Run to 1,000 steps, then continue the same trajectories to 2,000 to test catch-up. Only the 1,000-step endpoint selects settings or decides the primary comparison. Use test documents only for locked confirmation.

Record deployed validation CE and first-move accuracy throughout, steps and elapsed training time to the historical 66% accuracy target and to CE ≤ 0.60, the C01 final quality gate, and first-move test accuracy. For time-to-quality, count batch preparation plus updates, excluding setup, validation, probes, and warm-up. Record update-only time separately. Measure warmed single-update CPU tensor allocator peak above entry independently of persistent state and timing. Report paired costs with missed targets explicitly.

At fixed steps, inspect the current state and the next actual training batch without advancing its RNG. Pool coefficients across both layers. Record mean stiffness, mean absolute displacement, magnitudes of the signed elastic contribution and the scaled drive, their L2 ratio and cosine. On validation inputs compare fast-weight CE at the current state, after drive alone, and after drive plus restoration (same weights and batch). This single-step counterfactual tests whether restoration removes useful progress. The second horizon distinguishes an early lag from a gap persisting over the observed budget.

Judge full spring against the selected simpler moving anchor on fresh validation data. Call an advantage repeatable if all three corpus means favor spring and at least seven of nine paired runs favor it for final CE, first-hit steps or CPU time to equal CE. Report effect sizes, failures, the lightweight comparator, and trade-offs even for small effects. Missing hits cannot count as wins against a method that hits. Keep the architecture fixed and recommend disabling the coupled mechanism if it gives no repeatable advantage. Repeat scientific trajectories and verify agreement independently of timings.
